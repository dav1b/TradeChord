# Voice and Principles

## Governing idea

DataJockey sells judgment, not dashboards. A dashboard hands the reader everything and makes them find the story. The product is the confidence to make a decision and defend it. Every dashboard built under this brand must make a judgment legible — not just expose data.

## Tone

Calm, clear, confident. Plain English. One clear idea at a time.

## Named enemies (do not evoke their visual or verbal patterns)

- Freelance BI consultants
- Boutique data agencies
- Self-serve tools / dashboard theatre

## Copy discipline
- The copy in a single phrase: use language a professional can appreciate but a layman can understand.
- Plain questions over generic prompts: "What's not working?" beats "Describe your issue."
- Errors explain, never blame.
- Headlines carry edge; body copy carries credibility. Example pattern from the brand book:
  - Generic/overstated (avoid): copy that describes empowerment or data-driven decision-making in abstract terms.
  - Owned (use): direct, plain-English statements about trusting numbers and acting on them.
- One call to action per screen. Never two competing.
- No parallel aphoristic structures. No rhetorical scaffolding. No AI-tell symmetry (matched clause pairs, rule-of-three padding).

## UI copy rules

- Field labels and placeholders ask plain questions, not commands.
- Disabled/auto-filled states get a plain explanatory microcopy line, not silence.
- Error states explain what's wrong in one line, without blame language.

## Proprietary vocabulary (use consistently, don't paraphrase away)

Clarity debt, decision surface, decision-grade, judgment over tooling, dashboard theatre.

## Things to never do in generated dashboard copy

- Marketing-speak headers ("Unlock insights," "Empower your team")
- Hedged, uncertain framing on findings the data supports
- Two competing CTAs on one screen
- Decorative icons or illustration that doesn't carry information

## Voices we are inspired by

- Ernest Hemingway. Short phrases with powerful words.
- George Orwell. Never use a big words when a little word will do.
- Rory Sutherland. The art of the contrarian counter-intuitive yet profoundly true insight
